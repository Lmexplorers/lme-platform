WOODLAND DREAMS SHORT VEST

LME BABY COLLECTION - WOODLAND DREAMS

A short, open-front vest with wooden buttons, knitted to be worn over the Woodland Dreams basisbody. Worked flat from neck to hem, no sleeves, simple shoulder-line shaping and a garter stitch border around the neck, hem and armholes. Seven sizes, from 0-1 to 18-24 months. The vest is an accessory piece, and is knitted in one of the collection's colourful shades: sage green, dusty rose, butter yellow, soft blue, olive or terracotta, as a lovely contrast to the body's calm neutral colours.

ABOUT THE WOODLAND DREAMS SHORT VEST

PART OF THE COLLECTION

The short vest is one of six accessory pieces in LME Woodland Dreams, knitted to be worn over the basisbody. Where the body is the quiet, neutral foundation, the vest is the colourful layer lifted on top, a simple, open, sleeveless vest, easy to put on and take off, and lovely to combine with the collection's other pieces.

STYLE

Clean lines, a visible button band down the front and a simple garter stitch border around the neck, hem and armholes. No sleeves, no complicated details, just a small, well-fitting vest that adds an extra splash of colour over the body's calm tones.

A ROOMY FIT, MEANT TO GO OVER

The vest is knitted with a little extra width beyond the body's own chest measurement, so it sits nicely on top without pulling tight. It is clearly shorter than the body itself, meant to stop well above the crotch, not cover the whole body, see the size chart for exact measurements and the ratio to the body's own length.

GAUGE AND DIFFICULTY LEVEL

GAUGE

22 stitches and 30 rows in stockinette stitch = 10 x 10 cm, on 4 mm needles, the same gauge as the basisbody. This gauge is used in every calculation in the pattern. Always knit a swatch first: cast on 26 stitches, work stockinette stitch for approx. 12 cm, bind off and block lightly. If your gauge does not match, change to a smaller or larger needle until it does, not just to match the yarn.

DIFFICULTY LEVEL

Easy to medium, simpler than the basisbody. The vest is worked flat throughout, with no rounds and no raglan sleeves. You should be comfortable increasing and decreasing, picking up stitches along an edge, and working simple buttonholes.

TIPS
  • The vest is worked flat throughout, never in the round. Turn your work at the end of every row, exactly as you are used to from ordinary flat knitting.
  • Use two stitch markers for the two shoulder lines, it makes it much easier to keep track of where the increases should happen.
  • Count your stitches at every checkpoint in the tables. It is easier to fix a mistake 10 rows back than 40.

HOW THE VEST IS CONSTRUCTED

The vest is knitted top-down, flat in one piece, with no sleeves. Five simple parts, in this order:

1. The neck edge
Cast on flat at the neck, work a few rows of garter stitch.
2. Shoulder-line shaping
Increase evenly along two shoulder lines (front-back and back-front) until the yoke width is reached.
3. Divide for the armholes
Bind off a few stitches on each side for the armholes, dividing into front-left, back and front-right.
4. Down to the hem
Knit each of the three panels straight down, finishing all with garter stitch at the hem.
5. The button band
Work/pick up a garter stitch band along both front edges, with buttonholes on one side.

PART 1: THE NECK EDGE

The vest is cast on at the neck and knitted flat, top-down, the whole way, since the front stays open from neck to hem. Work the first few rows in garter stitch before switching to stockinette stitch and starting the shoulder shaping.

Example, 0-1 months: Cast on 44 stitches flat on a 4 mm circular needle (you work back and forth, not in the round, throughout the whole vest). Work 4 rows of garter stitch (knit every row). On the last garter row, place a stitch marker after stitch 11 (the boundary between front-left and back), and another stitch marker after stitch 33 (the boundary between back and front-right).

The exact stitch count for your size is in the grading table on the next page.

PART 2: SHOULDER-LINE SHAPING

Switch to stockinette stitch. Now increase evenly along the two shoulder lines, until the yoke width is reached. The vest has no sleeves, so there are only two shoulder lines to increase along, not four raglan lines like on the basisbody. The construction is the same for every size, only the numbers change, see the grading table below.

Increase row (every 2nd row, always on the right side): increase 2 stitches right before and 2 stitches right after each of the two shoulder markers (8 stitches increased in total per increase row, because with only two lines instead of four, each line needs to increase a little faster to reach the same yoke width in the same number of rows). Knit plain stockinette, with no increases, on the row in between. Front-left therefore only increases along its one inner edge (the front stays straight along the button band edge itself), while the back increases along both of its edges.

PART 3: DIVIDE FOR THE ARMHOLES

Now the yoke is divided into three parts: front-left, back and front-right, with a small armhole bound off on each side. Do this the same way for every size, use your own row's stitch numbers from the table on the previous page.

Example with numbers for 0-1 months, use your own numbers from the table on the previous page.

  • Knit across the 11 front-left stitches (the stitches from the edge to the left shoulder marker).
  • Bind off 12 stitches for the armhole (these stitches sit right at the underarm).
  • Knit across the 22 back stitches (between the two shoulder markers, minus the stitches just bound off).
  • Bind off 12 stitches for the second armhole.
  • Knit across the remaining stitches for front-right.
  • You now have three separate panels on the needle: front-left and front-right with 27 stitches each, and back with 54 stitches. From here on each panel is worked separately, flat, with its own ball or strand of yarn, straight down to the hem.

The stitches bound off on each side are split evenly: half are counted from the front panel, half from the back panel, right where they meet at the shoulder marker. This is why front-left/front-right goes from 33 stitches (at yoke depth) down to 27 stitches (after dividing), and back goes from 66 stitches down to 54 stitches, see the grading table on the next page for your own size.

PART 5: THE BUTTON BAND AND BUTTONHOLES

Finally, work a simple garter stitch band along both straight front edges, with buttonholes on one side.

Example with numbers for 0-1 months, use your own numbers from the table on page 4 and the grading table on page 9.

  1. 1
    Pick up approx. 32 stitches along the whole front edge, from the hem up to the neck, with the right side facing you. Use the rule of thumb "pick up approx. 3 stitches for every 4 rows" as a starting point, adjust slightly if the edge draws in or ripples.
  2. 2
    Work garter stitch (knit every row) for 4-5 rows.
  3. 3
    On the buttonhole side (right front for girls, left front for boys, or whichever side suits best): on row 2, work 3 buttonholes, evenly spaced along the edge (bind off 2 stitches for each buttonhole, cast on 2 new stitches over the gap on the next row).
  4. 4
    Bind off all stitches loosely in garter stitch. Repeat on the other front edge, with no buttonholes.
  5. 5
    Sew 3 wooden buttons onto the button side, directly opposite each buttonhole.

The number of buttons and how many stitches you pick up along the front edge increase with the size, see the table on page 4 and the stitch counts in the grading table on page 9 (the same row count is used to calculate the front edge pickup). Space the buttons evenly: the first and last button approx. 1.5 cm from the neck and hem, the rest evenly spaced between.

THE ARMHOLE EDGE

The armhole gets the same simple garter stitch border as the rest of the vest, so it doesn't curl and sits nicely around the body's own sleeve underneath.

Pick up stitches all the way around the armhole (along the bound-off edge at the bottom and the two straight sides up towards the shoulder) with the right side facing you, approx. 46 stitches in total for your size (see the table below). Work 3-4 rows of garter stitch. Bind off loosely. Repeat the same way for the other armhole.

FINISHING

  1. 1
    Sew the two shoulder seams: place front-left and back, and front-right and back, right sides together, and sew a neat seam along the shoulder line (where the shoulder markers sat during part 2).
  2. 2
    Pick up and work the garter stitch border around both armholes, see the previous page.
  3. 3
    Pick up and work the button band with buttonholes along one front edge, and a plain garter band with no holes along the other, see part 5.
  4. 4
    Sew the buttons on directly opposite the buttonholes.
  5. 5
    Weave in all loose ends securely on the wrong side, and trim them short.
  6. 6
    Lightly steam-block the vest on the wrong side, avoid pressing directly on the garter stitch borders, they should keep their neat, non-curling structure.
  7. 7
    Finally, check that every button is securely attached, and that no loose threads or stitches can come undone.

SAFETY AND CARE

SAFETY
  • Always use small, securely attached buttons, and sew them on with double thread, several times through each hole.
  • Check the buttons regularly, especially after washing, and reattach them at the first sign of looseness.
  • This pattern is not intended for children who put small objects in their mouth unsupervised, never leave a baby alone and unsupervised in the vest during the first while of wearing it.
  • All measurements and stitch counts in this pattern are calculated for a roomy, comfortable fit, not a tight safety fit, always follow current safety recommendations for children's clothing.
CARE INSTRUCTIONS

Hand wash gently in lukewarm water with wool wash, or machine wash on a wool cycle at 30 degrees. Press out the water, ease into shape, and dry lying flat on a towel. Avoid hanging the vest up to dry, alpaca can stretch out of shape.
